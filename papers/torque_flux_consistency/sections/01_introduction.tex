
\section{Question: can torque reveal spatially implausible flux structure?}
A stationary voltage reconstruction can produce a dq flux map that is locally
noisy or wavy even when its broad magnetic trend is credible. An independently
measured torque channel supplies a second physical view of the same operating
points. The question of this paper is whether the \emph{spatial structure} of
their disagreement can regularize the map without pretending that shaft torque
equals electromagnetic torque point by point. Whole-map identification under
resistance and inverter uncertainty illustrates why voltage reconstruction
requires explicit nuisance treatment \cite{liu2018fluxmap}; torque-assisted
parameter estimation likewise exists, but does not remove the field-level
nullspace studied here \cite{wang2026torque}.

Picture two surfaces above the $(i_d,i_q)$ plane: measured torque and torque
calculated from the reconstructed flux. At one speed, thermal state and machine
configuration, friction, nearly constant loss contributions and sensor offset
may shift the surfaces vertically. Such a shift is not the magnetic defect of
interest. Different local waviness is. We therefore seek a small, smooth global
flux correction that makes the torque difference spatially flat while fitting
one nuisance offset per comparable operating-condition group.

This changes the role of observability. Torque's rank-one projection and radial
nullspace do not end the reconstruction attempt; they explain why regularization
and explicit physical priors are necessary and which part remains unverified.
The contribution is the residual hypothesis, its global objective, a known-truth
recovery and a qualified portable two-speed demonstration. Symmetry projection,
integrability testing, general co-energy fitting and multi-error identification
remain owned by the companion papers rather than being duplicated here.
