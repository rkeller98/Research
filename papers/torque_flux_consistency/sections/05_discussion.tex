\section{Limitations and interpretation boundary}
The method needs a torque channel independent of the voltage reconstruction,
but the portable CAN signal is not a certified electromagnetic reference.
Mechanical friction, windage and sensor offset may look approximately constant
inside a group. Iron loss, harmonic loss and current-dependent loss need not;
the constant $c_g$ is therefore falsifiable and should be replaced only by a
cross-validated low-complexity nuisance model when evidence requires it.

The stationary dq equation reconstructs terminal-current flux. In a loss-aware
equivalent circuit the magnetic storage current can differ, so applying the
same torque law to terminal coordinates needs justification. Voltage offsets,
dead time, used resistance, speed error, angle error and unequal representation
of the two phase systems can all imprint spatial residual structure. A fitted
flux correction can absorb them without identifying their cause.

Torque observes only one local projection. Smoothness, minimum change,
reciprocity and any bounds select the unobserved component by assumption. The
radial potential counterexample remains even for smooth reciprocal fields with
positive added curvature. Near zero current normalized torque-to-flux residuals
amplify noise and are undefined at the origin; the global objective can retain
raw torque units instead of inserting an arbitrary denominator epsilon.

The synthetic experiment validates implementation against known truth, not an
experimental machine. The real experiment has one PSM lineage, two speeds and
one temperature reference. Its in-sample reduction is expected from fitting;
the failed leave-one-speed-out transfer is the stronger scientific result.
No corrected real map is promoted to product use, and no automatic MeasEval
integration follows from this research demonstration.
