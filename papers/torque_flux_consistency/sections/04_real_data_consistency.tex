\section{A configuration-qualified real torque comparison}
\label{sec:real-torque}
The portable fixture \texttt{psm\_dual\_system\_multirpm} contains 218 OPs,
109 each at requested 1000 and 3000 rpm, a 70-$^\circ$C rotor reference,
$p=4$ and a recorded nominal stator resistance of 10 m$\Omega$.
The stored phase selector is 1. Its import/test interpretation identifies
two three-phase systems with a 180-degree displacement.
For equally represented per-system amplitude-invariant dq currents and flux,
the total torque is the sum of their oriented-area contributions:
\begin{equation}
 M_{\rm total}=2\left[\frac32p(\psi_di_q-\psi_qi_d)\right],
 \qquad k_{\rm T}=\frac62p=3p=12.
 \label{eq:dual-system-torque}
\end{equation}
This factor follows from configuration, not a regression on CAN torque.
The old single-system value 6 omitted half the represented total torque.
With a shared $I_s\ge0.1I_{s,\max}$ support, the combined RMS discrepancy
falls from 19.71 to 1.21 Nm when that configuration is applied.
Those figures are recomputed from the portable export using measured mean
electrical speed; they do not reuse the previous requested-speed audit.

The raw-to-OP route retains measured current/voltage means, electrical-speed
means, requested labels, medians, deviations and counts. No torque outlier
deletion or fitted gain is applied. A median phase-RMS to dq-magnitude ratio
near 0.706 agrees with the importer's amplitude-invariant interpretation;
zero power-analyzer voltage channels supply no independent voltage check.
Changing configured pole pairs alone cannot fix the previous factor: in a
requested-speed voltage inversion $k_T/\omega_e$ contains cancelling $p$.
The two-system factor, by contrast, survives that ratio.

\subsection{Why this candidate and what remains unqualified}
The raw catalog and candidate audit compare quiet PSM, merge, EESM, ASM,
torque-characterization and Outlier cases. Some candidates have smaller
within-group scatter but held or repeated CAN values do not prove independent
sensor precision. Multi-RPM supplies both current directions, both torque
signs, complete voltage/current channels, explicit phase configuration and
two actual speed slices with a narrow repeat-scatter scale. It is the primary
configuration diagnostic. The noisy Outlier lineage is retained only as a
reduced stress fixture; edited PSM speed variants are excluded.

MeasEval transports CAN values unchanged and labels them torque; raw unit
metadata does not certify a calibrated transducer. Gain, gear ratio, shaft
location, magnetic versus terminal currents and mechanical losses are not
established. Hence the residual is a qualified signal comparison, not a
validated electromagnetic flux-error measurement. No real flux correction
is performed.
