\section{Conclusion}
An independently measured stationary torque supplies a useful consistency
condition through the spatial shape of measured-minus-theoretical torque.
Allowing one nuisance offset per comparable operating condition separates this
shape question from unjustified pointwise equality. A global minimum-change,
smooth correction can then suppress torque-visible flux structure; a co-energy
parameterization adds reciprocity as an explicit prior.

Known-truth recovery demonstrates substantial improvement while the preserved
radial counterexample shows exactly what torque cannot determine. The portable
two-speed data confirm the importance of offsets and the six-phase $k_T=3p$
qualification, but single-speed corrections do not transfer across speed.
Torque is therefore an independent consistency regularizer, not a complete
flux sensor and not yet a calibrated real-map correction for this dataset.
