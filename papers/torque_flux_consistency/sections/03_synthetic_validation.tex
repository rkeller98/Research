\section{Known-truth global recovery}
\label{sec:synthetic-torque}
The recovery experiment starts from the smooth reciprocal saturated co-energy
used by the companion numerical benchmarks. On a $17\times17$ grid with
$|i_d|,|i_q|\leq\SI{90}{\ampere}$, $p=3$ and $k_T=4.5$, its exact gradient is
the ground-truth flux. The raw map adds four controlled components: a smooth
conservative distortion, a spatially oscillating torque-normal wave, a radial
torque-null potential and independent \SI{0.5}{\milli\weber} point noise.
Synthetic measured torque is generated from truth with a \SI{1.2}{\newton\metre}
constant offset and \SI{0.12}{\newton\metre} standard-deviation noise.

The global fit uses \cref{eq:global-objective,eq:coenergy-correction}. One offset
and all potential coefficients are solved simultaneously. The reported
$\lambda=0.1$ is fixed before inspecting ground-truth flux error; truth evaluates
the result but does not tune it. The CSV output retains every truth, raw and
corrected vector, both torque residuals and the injected radial null component.

Flux RMSE falls from 4.636 to \SI{1.893}{\milli\weber}; centered torque-residual
RMS falls from 1.884 to \SI{0.211}{\newton\metre}. The estimated offset is
\SI{1.212}{\newton\metre}, close to the injected value. Torque-normal error
falls from 4.405 to \SI{0.584}{\milli\weber}. The fitted correction has reciprocal
mixed derivatives to roundoff.

The full-map second-difference RMS changes from 1.289 to 1.317 mWb rather than
decreasing. This is not a contradiction: the correction itself is smooth, but
torque cannot remove the injected radial point structure and a conservative
addition cannot cancel nonconservative curl. Smoothness of the chosen
correction and smoothness of the corrected map are distinct reported claims.

The counterexample is equally important. Repeating the same fit with and without
the injected radial potential gives identical fitted coefficients, and the
radial difference survives to $3.7\times10^{-14}$ mWb numerical error. Since a
conservative correction has zero curl, it also cannot cancel curl introduced by
the deliberately nonconservative wave and point noise. Reciprocity is therefore
a model-class choice, not evidence that every raw defect was repaired.

\subsection{Regularization and observability checks}
The generated sensitivity file reports flux RMSE, centered residual RMS,
correction magnitude and roughness for five decades of $\lambda$. The retained
58 local assertions cover weighted projection, torque offset/gain, current and
torque noise, resistance signs, loss terms, both current axes, all quadrants,
both torque and speed signs, the origin and small currents, joint voltage--torque
rank, excitation slices, the reciprocal radial potential and the fact that a
pointwise minimum-change correction need not be conservative.
