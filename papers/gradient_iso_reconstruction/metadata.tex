\title{Gradient-Iso Reconstruction from Scattered Samples:\\
Local Derivatives, Level-Set Geometry, and the Limits of Confidence}
\author{Raphael Keller}
\date{Research draft --- 7 October 2026}
\newcommand{\PaperAbstract}{%
Sparse scalar measurements can reveal local improvement directions without
a global surrogate. We develop affine interpolation on Delaunay simplices,
explain how geometry controls derivative error, and recursively lift the
derivative cloud to higher orders. A Python implementation reuses the VICE
\texttt{EdgeIntersector} and retains source-simplex provenance. Exact affine
tests, noise propagation, quadratic refinement, recursive curvature, and
synthetic contamination checks separate algebraic correctness from
statistical reliability. Clean first derivatives improve with refinement,
whereas fixed measurement noise is amplified. Recursive second derivatives
are not automatically symmetric or polynomial preserving. The historical
iso-density confidence interpretation fails an affine counterexample and
performs poorly as a low-score anomaly ranking in the reported experiments.
We retain iso coverage as a geometric descriptor, expose undefined plateau
cases, and re-evaluate proposed search steps using the true synthetic
objective. This yields a reproducible research method with explicit
assumptions and limitations.}
