\section{Question: what survives outside the ideal symmetry?}
A reconstructed direct-axis flux map need not give the same value at positive
and negative quadrature current. Saturation, coordinate alignment, electrical
losses, and reconstruction uncertainty must be distinguished before such a
difference can be interpreted. Saturation within a reflection-symmetric
quasi-static magnetic law changes the allowed flux structure; it does not by
itself create its forbidden reflection components.

The research question is: \emph{What information is contained in the forbidden
symmetry components of experimentally reconstructed dq flux-linkage maps, and
to what extent can a simple resistance-mismatch hypothesis explain their
spatial, thermal, and speed-dependent structure?} The contribution is an
evidence-preserving interpretation of two supplied test-bench datasets,
supported by explicit error models and reproducible numerical checks.
It is not a claim to have invented stator-resistance identification or to
have uniquely corrected the measured machine parameters.

\subsection{Position relative to existing identification methods}
Stationary rotor-oriented voltage balances are standard
\cite{binder2017,schroeder2021}. Liu et al. identify complete flux-linkage maps
under uncertain circuit resistance and inverter nonlinearity \cite{liu2018};
their position-offset method uses deliberate positive and negative offsets
for parameter determination under different loads \cite{liu2017}.
Neither is equivalent to passively identifying an unknown encoder offset
from the two residual channels studied here.
Sun and Xiao explicitly discuss nonlinear map parity, continuous differential
inductances, and equal differential cross-inductances \cite{sun2020}.
Energy-based modeling provides a complementary explanation of reciprocity
and construction symmetry \cite{jebai2014}.

Motoring/generating combinations are established tools: Richter et al.
match corresponding magnetic states while accounting for iron-loss currents
\cite{richter2014}; Varvolik et al. average opposite modes to reduce the
influence of resistive and inverter voltage terms in magnetic identification
\cite{varvolik2022}. The present analysis retains the complementary
half-difference and half-sum that would disappear under ideal symmetry
projection. Requested-current mirror pairs do not establish equal magnetic
states in a loss-containing machine.
Brescia et al. identify resistance, flux, inductance and an inverter term
from selected steady operating conditions \cite{brescia2023}; this stresses
the relevance of current, speed, temperature and nuisance assumptions.
Nonlinear loss-aware synchronous-machine models also distinguish magnetic
and loss effects \cite{hackl2021,kruener2019}. These works motivate candidate
causes, rather than proving a cause for the supplied measurements.

\subsection{Evidence and reading conventions}
The paper first defines reconstruction and the symmetry baseline, then derives
resistance and angle fingerprints. The experimental sections document the
existing data reduction, display unsmoothed residual structure, and compare
the constant-resistance hypothesis across current, rotor reference and speed.
Conditional identifiability and retained synthetic checks explain what extra
information a causal calibration would require. The discussion deliberately
leaves the physical decomposition open.

Unadorned quantities denote truth. Hats denote measured coordinates, measured
signals or a used parameter, specified at introduction.
$\flux(\current)$ is a constitutive map; $\flux^{\rm rec}$ is reconstructed
from voltage. Map comparisons use the same numerical current argument.
Angles are electrical radians; speed is signed electrical angular speed.
The symmetry baseline holds thermal and magnet state fixed and presumes
consistent harmonic averaging. Experimental thermal labels are references,
not verified winding temperatures. The two files are analyzed separately;
their different stored pole-pair constants preclude assuming a single common
machine calibration without additional provenance.
