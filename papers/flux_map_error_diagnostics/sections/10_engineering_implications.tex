\section{What a supported correction would require}
The experimental equivalent summaries are not treated as independently
identified corrections of the supplied maps. Their operating-point and speed
dependence contradicts the unrestricted
application of a single constant-resistance correction. A correction would
need independent support for its physical interpretation: calibrated terminal
voltages, thermal state, current matching, a magnetic reference for angle
sensitivity, and validation on separate operating regions and speeds.
The conservation cross-check additionally shows that a hypothetical adjustment
can improve selected parity while worsening global and path metrics.
Raw measured signals, raw reconstructed maps and hypothesis-adjusted results
must remain separately available; equivalent summaries remain diagnostics.
A visible tilt can resemble resistance mismatch without identifying it.
Symmetry-based untilting can also remove information relevant to a loss-aware
analysis of matched magnetic states \cite{companion-coenergy}; the present
experiments do not identify iron-loss currents. That possibility reinforces
preserving the raw residual rather than treating every forbidden component
as an independently correctable error.
The parameter-rank argument above is conditional on these assumptions; it is
not an experimental identification result for either supplied file.

For completeness, retain the inverse transformation verified on the known
synthetic model. If independent evidence identifies
$\alpha=\widehat{\delta\gamma}$ and $\widehat{\varepsilon_R}$, corrected labels
satisfy $\hat\current=\rotationmatrix(-\alpha)\current_c$, and
\begin{equation}
 \flux_c(\current_c)=\rotationmatrix(\alpha)
 \left[\hat\flux(\hat\current)
 -\frac{\widehat{\varepsilon_R}}{\omegae}\rotationgenerator\hat\current\right].
 \label{eq:corrected-map}
\end{equation}
Subtracting the reconstruction bias, rotating its vector, and relabeling the
current argument inverts \cref{eq:combined-exact} under its model assumptions.
The proposed resistance would be $\hat R_s-\widehat{\varepsilon_R}$ and the
proposed angle $\hat\gamma-\widehat{\delta\gamma}$. Correcting only curve
ordinates while leaving current labels unchanged would not invert an angle
offset. Originals and assumptions must remain available for checking any
such interpretation.

In the known-model noiseless combined synthetic case, this operation reduces
the maximum normalized residual norm from $0.07023$ to
$2.154\times10^{-6}$. This verifies the implementation and sign convention;
it supplies no measured correction accuracy. A current-proportional voltage
error can produce the same apparent improvement under a resistance
interpretation, as \cref{eq:confounding} demonstrates. Smaller residuals alone
cannot establish the identified physical parameter.
